Fraud screening systems must rank suspicious transactions quickly and tell the analyst why a transaction was flagged. Graph neural networks (GNNs) exploit transaction relations but usually rely on post-hoc explainers, which are costly and may not reflect the model. We propose RTXGNN, a self-explaining temporal GNN whose learned feature, edge and node masks gate message passing and are returned as the explanation of every score. A training objective makes the top-$k$ explanation sufficient for the prediction and its complement uninformative, and a shift-invariant temporal encoding (HRAPE) supplies time gaps, recency and transaction velocity. On the Elliptic Bitcoin dataset with a temporal split and 16 baselines over 10 seeds, RTXGNN reaches a test F1 of \rtxFone{} and an average precision of \rtxAP{}, on par with the strongest GNN baselines and higher on average than the self-explaining SEFraud (\deltaFOnesefraud{} F1). Gradient-boosted trees on the same engineered features remain the most accurate model (F1 \xgbFone). RTXGNN's ranking reaches a precision of \rtxPrecAtFive{} among the 500 highest-scored test transactions. Its explanations are nearly as sufficient as Integrated Gradients at about one hundredth of the cost, and far more stable than GNNExplainer. On a synthetic benchmark with planted laundering rings, the most important incoming transaction of a detected ring member is a ring transaction in \ringHitPct\% of the cases. Scoring and explaining a transaction end to end takes \rtxLatBoneP{} ms at the 99th percentile on one CPU core. We also analyse the temporal encoding, drift and retraining, label efficiency and a second financial dataset.
