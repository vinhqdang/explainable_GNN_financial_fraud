Fraud screening systems must rank suspicious transactions quickly and tell the analyst why a transaction was flagged. Graph neural networks (GNNs) exploit the relations between transactions but usually rely on separate post-hoc explainers, which are costly and may not reflect the model. We propose RTXGNN, a self-explaining temporal GNN. Learned feature, edge and node masks gate its message passing and are returned as the explanation of every score. A training objective makes the top-$k$ explanation sufficient for the prediction and its complement uninformative. A shift-invariant temporal encoding (HRAPE) supplies time gaps, recency and transaction velocity. On the Elliptic Bitcoin dataset with a temporal split, we compare against 16 baselines over 10 seeds. RTXGNN reaches a test F1 of \rtxFone{} and an average precision of \rtxAP{}. This matches the strongest GNN baseline and exceeds the self-explaining SEFraud by \deltaFOnesefraud{} F1. Gradient-boosted trees on the same engineered features remain the most accurate model (F1 \xgbFone). For decision support, its ranking reaches a precision of \rtxPrecAtFive{} among the 500 highest-scored test transactions. RTXGNN's explanations are nearly as sufficient as Integrated Gradients at about one hundredth of the cost, and far more stable than GNNExplainer. On a synthetic benchmark with planted laundering rings, the edge explanations identify the ring transactions (top-ranked in \ringHitPct\% of detected members). On both Elliptic and synthetic data, HRAPE outperforms a Time2Vec encoding of the same time gaps. Scoring and explaining a transaction end to end takes \rtxLatBoneP{} ms at the 99th percentile on one CPU core. We also analyse drift and retraining, label efficiency and a second financial dataset.
